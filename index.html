The tool stack takes its cut before the client does

A side gig picks up software the way a van picks up roof racks: a project board at $12, invoicing at $15, file storage at $10, scheduling at $8, e-sign at $11. Each one is a rounding error next to a client check, so each one survives its own review. Stacked, they are a $56 monthly line that bills through the dead months exactly like the busy ones.

Priced as an hourly line it moves with volume, not with effort. Spread across 60 billable hours, the stack costs $0.93 an hour on a $25 rate, under 4%. The same stack across a 15-hour month costs $3.73 an hour, nearly 15% of the rate. The work did not get worse and the client paid the same; the fixed costs repriced every hour sold.

The break-even is the useful number. Keeping software under 5% of a $25 rate means 56 divided by $1.25 is 44.8, so the stack needs 45 billable hours a month to earn its shelf. Under that line the honest move is dropping tools, not hunting clients: invoicing and e-sign carry the first month alone, and a board can join the week the calendar actually fills.
